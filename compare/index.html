Finder, or Vista?

Finder is built into macOS and is the right tool for plenty of jobs. Vista is a visual file browser for the same files: larger thumbnails, search inside what’s in them, and folders you can shape. Here is how they differ.

Features compared between Vista and Finder
Feature Vista Finder
Visual browsing with large thumbnails Files and folders as large thumbnails, sorted into photos, screenshots, documents and videos, with file counts and storage totals. Icon, list, column and gallery views; icon size and previews are set per view.
Search inside files and screenshots One search reads file names and the words inside screenshots, images, PDFs and documents (plain text, Markdown, RTF, Word, Pages). Results show the matching line and jump to the PDF page. Spotlight searches names and indexed text in some documents. It doesn’t read the words inside screenshots or images.
Visual Search (describe a photo or video) Describe a photo or video and Vista finds it. The model runs on your Mac after a one-time download, then works offline. No. Spotlight matches names and metadata, not what’s in a picture.
Folder customization (colors, shapes, icons) 24 colors, 7 rounder folder shapes and 31 icons. Apply one style, or varied colors, to every folder at once. Color tags, and custom icons one folder at a time.
Clean Up (preview and Undo) Suggests old downloads, leftover installers, duplicates and near-identical photos. Review before anything moves; files go to the Trash, and Undo brings them back. No built-in duplicate or near-identical cleanup; you sort files yourself or use another app.
Themes (Sage, White, Dark) Sage is the default, with White and Dark in the theme picker. Switch any time. Follows the system light or dark appearance; no per-app theme picker.
Privacy (everything stays on your Mac) Indexing, text recognition, duplicate detection and Visual Search run on your Mac. No account, no cloud, no tracking. Files are local; iCloud Drive syncs them if you turn it on.
Keyboard shortcuts Arrow keys and single-key shortcuts move between categories and files; / to search, g then a letter to jump, and ? for the shortcut list. Open Vista from the menu bar. Extensive built-in shortcuts, plus Quick Look with Space.

Compared on macOS 14 Sonoma. Finder features vary by macOS version and settings. Vista requires macOS 14 or later.

Can I use both?

Yes. Vista runs alongside Finder and works with the folders you already have. It doesn’t take over your desktop, change your file types, or ask you to move anything. Open Vista when a folder is easier to understand as pictures; keep Finder for everything else.
